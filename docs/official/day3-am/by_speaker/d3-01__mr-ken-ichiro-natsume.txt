Many of you joining us now have been in this building or online over the past two days for our inaugural global forum on IP and AI. Two days of incredible dialogue on legal and policy issues at the intersection of IP and AI.
It’s not a coincidence that those two events are taking place over three consecutive days. They reflect two different aspects of the same broad set of issues, how best to ensure that the IP system remains fit for purpose in the era of AI with human at the heart of the system and AI used as a tool to empower and support human creativity and innovation, not to replace it. In July at the General Assembly WIPO Director General described WIPO’s work on AI and IP as car with four wheels policy, technical infrastructure, implementation and capacity building. The global forum on Monday and Tuesday were the policy wheel with some moments to reflect on implementation. Today is a technical infrastructure wheel.
A car is fitting analogy for WIPO’s approach to AI because a car cannot run on one wheel. It needs all four moving in unison. Infrastructure is often the wheel that is treated as an afterthought until something goes wrong, but very important. On the policy side Member States continue to evaluate and make policy choices through their own national and regional processes. As we have heard on Monday from Director General Tan and throughout the discussion at the global forum we may need not only new IP rights but new approaches to the broader administration of IP system.
As those policy discussions continue and decisions are made, ultimately it will be the role of IP infrastructure to turn those policy choices into practical and operational reality for the participants of the IP ecosystem. Already on the infrastructure side, Generative AI is putting pressure on systems and tools that specific industries need for specific uses. While the policy discussions are playing out, right holders, creators, innovators and AI developers alike all have to find ways to operate now. As the Director General noted on Monday, the path to making the economics of IP in the area of AI work requires workable ways for people to say yes. Because law alone will not solve every problem.
This is where I triple I comes in, to leverage WIPO’s unique position as a global neutral expert agency that can convene conversations and collaborations and bring those infrastructure issues to the fore. I triple I is not just about copyright. Copyright infrastructure is the best known area of IP infrastructure, although even then it is likely not well known enough. And AI raises infrastructure questions across all of IP. For example, AI is increasingly used as a tool in the innovation process itself.
You will hear later a discussion of exactly why and how the notion of AI generated content is playing out in the innovation context and why we cannot just think of copyright. When the DG described the four wheels of AI and IP power in July, this wheel was already turning. We launched already in March and the technical exchange network or TEN, our global collection of non-government experts at the heart of the triple I has been at work since. You will hear about some of the details of the TEN shortly, but I want to offer a note of thanks to all our TEN members for their very much engagement in this project. So active and lively discussions.
