Yes, because public funding in France can represent up to 50% of the production budget so producers really need it. And so the CNC which is the national center for cinema and they offer a lot of subsidies and they deliver film visas when you want to do a theatrical release. So they very recently updated the general regulations to make subsidies conditional to human involvement. So they have said and this is from last month, that it will not be possible to receive CNC funding for preproduction, development and production without providing evidence of the involvement of at least one writer for the writing or rewriting of the screenplay and the storyboard and one graphic designer for animated works. And the same goes for original music, for dubbing, subtitling, post synchronization, audio description and voiceover commentary.
They all need at least one human. So the requirement for human authorship is clear and it does leave space for AI because AI is very necessary in a production workflow to remain competitive. So producers now, they need to know ahead if they qualify for public funding and they don’t want to risk at the end of the production finding out that the production is not eligible anymore and they may need to reimburse some of the public funding because they have used too much AI or the wrong kind of AI. And in international coproductions especially which are really great cultural collaborations but producers need to be extra careful and pay attention to the AI use in their coproducing partner territory because they need to remain eligible in their home territory and different jurisdictions, they can take different approaches to AI disclosure but when it comes to AI labeling, it helps if we speak the same language.
