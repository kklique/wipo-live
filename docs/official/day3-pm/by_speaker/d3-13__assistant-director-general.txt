Now let me call on Mr. Harry Bolton, head of operations copyright licensing agency United Kingdom. You have the floor, sir.

Thank you, Chair. As said, my name is Will Kress, the Executive Director of HAND, the Universal Talent Identifier which is a non-profit registration agency of the DOI Foundation operating continuously since January 23. Much of TEN’s work concerns content and its provenance and while provenance record can tell us what happened to a file, it cannot on its own tell us who was in it and who can say yes to its use. When a person’s name, image, likeness or voice appears in an AI system, the question is not only is this synthetic, it is also which person is this and did they consent? And are they being credited and paid?
HAND’s position is simple. Human agency and creator primacy compensation cannot flow to a person the system cannot properly identify. That requires a neutral identity layer that is trusted, automated and measurable. HAND’s approach starts from evidence, public institutional record such as credits, contracts and editorial citations, never biometric capture. Today we identify only public figures in cultural industries.
For TEN, we offer a practical experience with persistent identifiers for people and their consent-based likenesses. We look forward to learning more about the planned work on personal characteristics. Whatever rights frameworks emerge, they will need to support identity layer. We are glad to have the digital database of IPTC and also active members of the CPA and the creative research working group. Thank you very much.

We recently published research with the Howard University Artificial Intelligence Initiative, the African Union, as well as the National Academy of Sciences. What we are building quite simply is applying DPI principles which is identification, secure data exchange and payment rails as well as building an additional layer of the infrastructure which is treaty and rights aware consent policies for Artificial Intelligence. Our theory is that technology is only as valuable as the culture that it can access and, therefore, it is imperative that we figure out ways for authors and institutions who create and steward these works to be dignified in how they are used and applied and how they travel through Artificial Intelligence Systems. We would be honored to support any working groups or pilots.
We actually have active pilots with some of the largest publishers in the world across music, university archives and historical estates which quite simply to revisit the earlier thesis is to dignify that author to say who ethically and commercially can access our work, under what terms and under what geographical treaties would we like the large language models and AI systems to be aware of when accessing this work. We would welcome the opportunity to support any engagements and share research from some of our pilots and active deployments. Thank you all so much.
