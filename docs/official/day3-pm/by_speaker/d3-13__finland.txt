I would like to warmly thank all the organizers here at WIPO for this event, all the ten Working Group members as well as the colleagues here present online as well for this day of intense work around AI infrastructure. A lot of this corresponds to the work of the CITF that I have presented earlier in this meeting. Actually, it was in the launch event in March. We have taken huge steps after that. One perspective is important for us as a policy level representative.
It is also important to recognize that as we take concrete steps forward. What is the role, the new role of the copyright policy officers following the generative AI development? This is what I would like to raise. For instance, in Finland, copyright policy lies with the Ministry of Education and Culture. In some other countries, the policy officers might be in the Ministry of Enterprise or Ministry of Justice.
Sometimes they also are in the IP offices or in copyright offices. But our role is normally normative and we are not really prepared for all these complex questions. Now, this is something I propose that we could have more discussion on, how can we ensure that we have more resources as policy officers to deal with these issues and that we can then support all the various players in the markets, especially the creators, the human contributors to our societies in the age of AI. So I hope that if we have some Member States here who would resonate to this type of work, I’m glad to discuss that. Thank you.
