So the work of this group has been driven by the recognition that increasingly stakeholders are required to make a binary decision about a piece of content where that decision depends on the role of AI in the creation of that piece of content. So, for example, is a work eligible for copyright protection? Does it comply with the platform’s policies? Is it eligible for an award?
Or what technology should be deployed to detect infringements? And the questions underlying these decisions, they are often framed in really simple terms. Is it AI generated or how much AI was used? But as we try and answer those questions, it becomes clear that the answers are not straightforward. AI might have generated the whole track or the whole video or it might have generated just one part.
It might have modified human created content or assisted with research among many other roles as we have heard. And maybe there was only one prompt or 100 prompts or a new model was trained or an existing model was modified, was fine-tuned. And these are all very different forms and extents of AI involvement and yet organizations often require these stakeholders to make very binary decisions without specifying exactly which forms or extent of AI involvement really matter. And stakeholders lack a shared language to talk amongst themselves to describe those differences in a consistent way. And this is a problem because the consequences of these real world binary decisions can be really quite significant.
And so the goal of this focus group has therefore been to develop a taxonomy that provides a shared framework for describing the different ways that AI can be involved in content creation. And it’s intended for use across content sectors by a range of stakeholders from lawyers, policy, makers, scientists, creators, and business leaders. And importantly, the taxonomy is not here to prescribe the outcomes of those decisions. It does not determine whether something qualifies for copyright detection or complies with the policy or is eligible for an award. But instead it provides a common language for these stakeholders to describe what happens during the creation of that piece of content so that they can then assess the facts and make their own decisions.
