# [d3-14] Closing Remarks
# 발표자: Mr. Pascal Faure — Deputy Director General, World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)  (speaker)
# Day 3 · 2026-11-11 · 예정 17:55-18:00 (Geneva) · 발언 1회 · 9단락 · 1,015단어 · 구분 방식: official

And I would really like to thank them for that. I believe it has allowed all of us whether we are diplomats, policymakers or legal experts or representatives of all stakeholders to understand why the infrastructure of Intellectual Property is what it is. It is important and it concerns us all. Today standards, metadata and technological tools are absolutely indispensable for the production and distribution of works in digital format. Similarly Intellectual Property offices around the world are leveraging technology and Artificial Intelligence.
They are using this infrastructure to improve the efficiency of processing patents, trademarks and designs. But with the advent of Artificial Intelligence, the volume of works and inventions used to train Intellectual Property models has increased as well as the volume of innovations generated by AI. This is reaching an unprecedented scale. This means that regardless of political choices, whether adopted at the national or regional level or at the international level, we absolutely need adequate Intellectual Property infrastructure. That is efficient to implement these choices and to make them operational.
So the TEN has been working tirelessly throughout the past year to categorize the various characteristic elements of this infrastructure from Intellectual Property to the era of AI as well as the opportunities and challenges it can help to realize. Our speakers today have explained to us that many standards, metadata and relevant tools already exist but others are still lacking and drawing up such an assessment is a first step. It is essential for guiding future work to build on what already exists rather than reinventing it. As we have seen today, the exchanges between the expert members of the TEN have also facilitated a sharing of knowledge and fruitful discussions between the various sectors and the different regions represented. I would like to emphasize or rather reiterate once again that the TEN’s mission is not to define policies nor to establish standards or to develop new standards.
On the contrary, the goal of the TEN is to promote exchanges between experts from diverse backgrounds and to inform those who define standards and develop norms. This includes metadata and other necessary tools in the age of AI. So many of you in the audience, whether here in Geneva or online are responsible for defining policies, standards and these norms at the intersection of Intellectual Property and Artificial Intelligence and we hope that today’s discussions and the presentations you have attended will have been useful to you in this task. We are also all particularly grateful to the Member States and observers who have spoken today and who have shared their comments and suggestions regarding the work of the TEN. This is very valuable in allowing the TEN to take into account the concerns of policymakers and of the relevant stakeholders.
I believe that the TEN has accomplished a truly considerable amount of work on issues that, let’s face it, are quite complex. In just over six months, I believe. So I would like to thank all the members of the TEN who have devoted their time to us and their expertise during this period. Without them, the work presented today would obviously not have come to fruition. But all of this is only the beginning of the journey and I believe there is still a long way to go beyond the work of the four working groups.
It is now important to create links between them in order to compliment the conclusions of each of these groups. The next step, therefore, is to identify and formalize the overlaps between these four axes so that the results obtained are based on the same terminology, the same taxonomies and the same use cases as was clearly explained earlier. Furthermore, and in light of what we have heard today, the work of IEEE is intended to expand into new fields and as TEN moves into new areas, the group will need additional expertise to support its work. Beyond that and in order to gather the perspectives of stakeholders from as diverse a range of backgrounds as possible, we would be delighted to welcome experts whose contributions could compliment the talents that have been brought together today. Membership in TEN remains open and we encourage all applications from qualified experts, particularly those from underrepresented sectors and regions.
This is to further enhance the diversity of the areas of expertise represented. Intellectual property is territorial by nature but AI is not. That is why a forum where these technical issues can be openly discussed at an international level should benefit everyone so that the necessary infrastructure can rely on common solutions or interoperable ones and that no one is left behind. Deputy Director General Ken Natsume and his colleagues have described the beginning of this journey as the road to AI Tripoli. I believe that following today’s meeting, this journey has indeed begun and we all sincerely hope that it will lead to concrete results on which industry, users and Member States can rely.
So to conclude this meeting, I would like, of course, to thank all the facilitators, the speakers and the contributors who have enabled us to draw the conclusions from the year’s work. I would also like to thank all my colleagues who have facilitated the work of the TEN throughout the year, in particular, of course, Gareth Levine and Ryan Groves who led the work with great energy and passion today’s session and without them, your work today would not have been possible. Finally, I would like to thank the AI. It has allowed us to express ourselves in our own language, including Japanese. I understand that it was given a special treatment but it is important to facilitate communication for the benefit of as many people as possible.
Finally, I wish the TEN to continue this important work in a spirit of dialogue and cooperation. That drives it. I sincerely hope that we can meet again next year to see the results of the work to come. Finally, I wish you all a safe journey home. Thank you very much.
