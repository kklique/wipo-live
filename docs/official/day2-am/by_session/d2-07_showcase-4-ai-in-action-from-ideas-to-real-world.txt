That was a fantastic and very lively discussions. For our final AI in action showcases we are taking you to Guinea where a young innovator is tackling a major public health challenge. In many communities diagnosing diseases such as malaria and tuberculosis remains difficult, particularly where laboratories and specialists expertise are scarce. But what if AI could bring diagnostic capabilities closer to the people who need them? Our next speaker, Mr. Alain Ephraim Gomu, a young innovator and entrepreneur from Guinea is developing an AI-powered diagnostic device designed to detect these diseases rapidly even without an Internet connection.
Alain, floor is yours.
Hello, everyone. I am very happy to be here at this global forum on Intellectual Property and in particular on artificial intelligence. My name is Alain Gomu. I come from Guinea, specifically in West Africa. Today I am going to talk to you about technology but before that I would like to tell you the story of Fatu.
Fatu is a little girl who was six years old. She lives with her parents in a small village in Guinea. One night Fatu falls ill. She has a fever. Her parents take her to the village clinic.
There, they realize that there is no electricity and the doctor explains to them that he won’t be able to diagnose what little Fatu is suffering from. So the doctor asks them to send Fatu to a hospital that is near the big city. It is located about 60 kilometers from the village. So that’s how Fatu’s father takes her to the hospital but unfortunately on the way, Fatu dies. She never makes it to the hospital because they couldn’t diagnose her in time.
These various diseases, particularly malaria and tuberculosis can be treated more quickly if we manage to diagnose them here but the reality is that it is very bitter in the field. According to the latest WHO studies, more than 610,000 deaths are due to mortality. We are affected in Africa in 2024. The vast majority of these are in Africa. When we look at the figures for tuberculosis as well, the numbers are also very alarming and as I was saying earlier, these two diseases can be treated but before we can treat them, we must be able to diagnose them in time.
However, in the field we find that the diagnosis comes too late. Why is that? Because access to diagnosis here is very, very complicated and also we have hospitals that lack electricity and without equipment and without a microscope. We really can’t make these different diagnoses. There are also rapid diagnostic tests, RDTs here but they are also limited in terms of reliability and as I was saying, there are various health centers in the villages that don’t have laboratories either.
So, they can’t make these diagnoses and even when we want to do them, it takes a tremendous amount of time. It takes days to get it done. It is based on these observations that we developed Colopuno AI. Here, it means health in the Fulani language. Fulani is a language spoken in Guinea.
So, what is Colopuno? Colopuno is a medical device that incorporates artificial intelligence. It allows for the diagnosis of, among other things, malaria and tuberculosis, specifically in less than five minutes. It operates without electricity. That means we don’t need a power source to run it.
It works using a solar battery and a solar panel. So, that means that even in a small village where there is no electricity at all, where there is nothing, we can still perform a diagnosis. Specifically, here, we are using artificial intelligence to see how it works. That is to say, the doctor here will try to take a blood sample. Here, we have two methods.
We have the thin smear and the thick drop. Once we have prepared these different biological samples, we scan them with the microscopic camera that is here and the AI will analyze the different patterns to see if there is a parasite or not. Here is the photo that was taken in less than five minutes. We can directly generate the medical report and this report can be used to treat the patient more quickly. So, that’s it.
Well, I don’t know if the video is working or not. So, that’s how it works. We place the slide and prepare it. We insert it here and the AI will take the different images and it will analyze the image and it can detect the parasites at the same time it can generate the report. Now, what could this change tomorrow in the village of Fatu?
That is to say, once we have this device, if later a child or a person like Fatu finds herself in this condition, when she arrives at the hospital, for example, even if there is no microscope or electricity available, we will be able to diagnose that person and that is exactly what we want to do with artificial intelligence. In other words, to save lives because AI is intelligent. That’s why. In particular, our project also involves AI but AI is at the heart of many things. It serves sustainable development, particularly according to the United Nations.
Here, AI will help us reduce various inequalities. In access to healthcare, that means, for example, a little girl living in a small village will have the same access to healthcare as a little girl in a big city and also in terms of clean solar energy, we will be able to diagnose more quickly. And who is this solution primarily for? That is to say, for health centers, clinics and laboratories. Currently, we are in the clinical phase.
In Guinea. And soon, we will move on to the pilot phase in Burkina Faso and Cameroon and Cote d’Ivoire. The goal is to eventually move towards certification and deployment in Africa. Here, since we are currently at the OMP, we have filed our patent in order to protect our algorithm model as well as our technology and also when we wanted to use the model for training, the various data we collected complied with the different rules because here, these are medical data that we use to train our model. We strictly adhere to them and all usage rights were respected to do this.
So I am almost at the end. Right now, we are in the middle of the development phase and research and all that. So whether you are a research center or an investor, we are open to discuss with you. I am Alain Gumu and I am the founder of Tina Tech because they say that with AI, no more children should die just because they are in a small village. They should have the same chances as those.
