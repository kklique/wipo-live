Still a human script. Thank you, Ken. And thank you to all of our speakers. Now, here’s a question that sounds deceptively simple. Who created it?
Who was the inventor? Since the inception of IP, that question has been central. An author writes a book. An inventor develops a new technology. A musician composes a song.
Of course, creativity and invention have never been entirely solitary activities. People use tools, draw on existing knowledge and build on the work of others. But AI is challenging some of the assumptions we made about the relationship between the person, the tool and the result. If an AI system helps produce a work or contributes to an invention, what kind of human contribution should matter? And how should we recognize that contribution with either overstating or diminishing the role of the human being?
Our next panel discussion, the human at the center, authorship and inventorship in the age of AI goes to the heart of that question. To guide us through the discussion, please welcome Mr. Mohamed El-Zent together with our distinguished panel. Mohamed and the panel, the floor is yours.
