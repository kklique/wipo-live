# [d2-10] Discussion: The IP in the Machine - Untangling IP Issues Behind the Models
# 발표자: Ms. Burcu G. Özcan Büyüktanır — Professor of Civil Law, Head of the Department of Civil Law, Director of the Health Law Research Center, Faculty of Law, Hacettepe University, Türkiye  (panelist)
# Day 2 · 2026-11-10 · 예정 15:00-16:00 (Geneva) · 발언 5회 · 9단락 · 610단어 · 구분 방식: official

Thank you. First of all, I would like to thank you for inviting me and for the organization. Coming to the question, we often focus on copyright, the use of protected works in AI training and patent law issues. AI innovation depends on technical development, yes, and access to data and data is lawful use. So in my view, protection data is important too because training data may include not only copyrighted works but also personal information, trade secrets.
So accession and usage of it must be lawful. And also we need innovation. So we need to strike a fair balance between encouraging innovations, protecting the right of others and safeguarding the public interest.

Well, I think we need to start by recognizing what each side needs. And then we need meaningful transparency and clear rules on when permission is required and when an exception may apply. And maybe we should also ask whether we need exceptions or we don’t need exceptions for using data. Thank you.

Thank you. I think existing Intellectual Property laws are important starting point for us but it’s the fact that now we are living with AI and we have new legal challenges. Therefore, I believe Intellectual Property law is inevitable that IP laws need to be updated. And our goal should be to establish a balanced legal framework for each sides of the issue. Let’s look at pharmaceutical patents.
Before the TRIPS as we know we did not have patent protection for pharmaceuticals and today we are discussing patent protection for personalized medicines that include genetic datas of the patients. So I’m afraid I think we will start to ask new questions about AI and IP law in a few years or we have been already asking. And let me say one more thing as a civil law professor. In any case related with AI, we should keep the human element in mind. If we leave the human element out of the legal regulations, this will also have direct and long-term implications for liability or AI-related harms, I think.

It depends on the type of innovation, actually. I think we should distinguish the protection of materials used in the training AI and protection of AI-related technical innovations. And maybe we can also say AI models themselves. But today under Turkish legal system, AI model itself is not generally patentable because we are looking for a technical solution for a technical problem. But if the AI is a part of the technical solution, then we can discuss patent protection of the AI with the invention.
I think again we will start to ask new questions about new perspectives for patent law, patent protection and as I mentioned before, we should keep the human element down on mind. Thank you. Thank you very much. But now I think that Korea offers a live experiment, claims to data structures and AI model structures were granted around 2019 but have been consistently rejected since 2020.

Thank you. My key message is maybe strike a balance. We need to strike a balance between encouraging inventions and with the protection of the rights of others and also we need, I think, fair competition in the market and so we need competition law with IP law also. And our aim should be to encourage innovation while protecting human creativity, individual rights and access to benefits of technology. And IP law should not only respond to this technological change and it should also help shape a fair, responsible innovation ecosystem while protecting the public interest.
Thank you very much to everyone for your interesting contributions and thank you all for your attention.
