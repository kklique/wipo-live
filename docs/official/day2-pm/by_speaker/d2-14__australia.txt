We have appreciated the discussions with other Member States and experts over the past two days. We have observed the great discussion on difficult legal and policy questions and how AI is already affecting IP systems in more practical ways. IP offices around the world are harnessing AI themselves and adapting their customers’ use of it as well. For Australia, this highlights the importance of maintaining two conversations in parallel. The first is the policy conversation about how IP frameworks respond to technological change and the second is the operational conversation about how IP offices can responsibly deploy AI to improve services, strengthen productivity and support users of the IP system.
Australia has found that practical implementation raises issues that extend well beyond the technology itself. Trust, governance, transparency, workforce capability and human oversight are just as important as the tools. As more offices deploy AI, we see significant value in sharing experiences, lessons learned, best practices and approaches to governance. We also see value in continuing international dialogue about the role of AI within the IP system while we also recognize that technologies and national approaches continue to evolve. The pace of technological change means none of us have all the answers.
What we do have is a shared interest and shared stewardship in ensuring that Intellectual Property systems remain trusted, effective and capable of supporting innovation and creativity in a rapidly changing world.
