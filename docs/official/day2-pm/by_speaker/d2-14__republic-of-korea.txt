At the same time, a common exploration has emerged. The IP system must adapt to rapid technological change while keeping human creativity, responsibility and dignity at its center. Building on Mr. Kim Yong-Sun’s remarks, allow me to offer three observations from Korea’s experience. First, human contribution and responsibility must remain central. Under Korea’s guidelines on AI assisted inventions, a human must make a substantial contribution to the invention to be recognized as an inventor.
An output generated by AI alone is not sufficient. The same principle should guide the use of AI within IP offices. AI can assist IP professionals, but legal judgments and final decisions must remain under meaningful human oversight. Second, AI should contribute not only to efficiency but also to the quality and integrity of the IP system. As AI lowers barriers to filing, IP offices face growing and application volumes and new challenges concerning the quality of applications.
Korea is responding through early screening measures and by piloting AI-powered patent search system, they can better understand the meaning and the technical context of patent documents. Third, successful AI transformation requires not only new tools but also new capabilities within IP offices. Korea has launched an internal AI app store through which staff can develop and share AI applications tailored to their work. This initiative demonstrates that successful transformation depends not only on technology but also on people, skills, trust, and organizational readiness. Mr. Chair, the national experiences shared at this forum reflect different practices but also common priorities they have made clear that no country or IP office can address these challenges alone.
The Republic of Korea supports WIPO’s leadership in developing shared principles for responsible AI use while respecting the different circumstances of IP offices. We also hope that WIPO will continue to serve as a global platform for practical cooperation and mutual learning. Finally, the we express our appreciation to the WIPO Secretariat, especially the IP and Frontier Technologies Division of the Infrastructure and Platform Sector for its dedicated efforts in making this forum possible. Thank you. Thank you very much.
Now I would like to call Ms. Lee Seung Min, Deputy Director of Copyright Policy Division, Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism, the Republic of Korea.

Thank you very much, Chair, for giving me the floor. The Republic of Korea sincerely congratulates WIPO on the historic opening of this global forum on IP and AI and expresses its deepest appreciation to the Director General and the WIPO Secretariat for their hard work in preparing this excellent event. We also thank the speakers and panelists who share their expertise on a wide range of cross-cutting issues concerning artificial intelligence and IP. The Republic of Korea is confident that this two-day forum has provided Member States, observers and stakeholders around the world with a valuable opportunity to seek common solutions to the issues arising at the intersection of artificial intelligence and IP. The Republic of Korea echoes the views that have been expressed today by experts from the creative industries, the publishing sectors and the legal profession during the session on human-centered creation and authorship, that creative contribution by human is the most important criteria.
Between 2024 and June of this year, the Republic of Korea published a total of four guides on AI and copyright, consistently stressing that the most important criterion of copyrightability is human creative contribution. Going forward in the legal global copyright discussions led by WIPO, the Republic of Korea sincerely believes that the essential elements of human creation should be treated in a technology-neutral manner, even amid swift technological innovations including artificial intelligence. Last but not least, the Republic of Korea would like to bring WIPO’s attention to various legal debates arising from mass acquisition of printed books for AI training which seem to have been occurring in many Member States including the Republic of Korea. The Republic of Korea hopes that meaningful discussion will take place among Member States within WIPO, which is the most authoritative forum for this agenda. Thank you very much, Chair.
