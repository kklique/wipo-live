Over these two days, we have explored a wide spectrum of AI issues across IP rights, IP systems, and IP offices. This global forum successfully fostered deeper engagement among Member States, industry, creators, technology companies, and other stakeholders so that we can develop policies and solutions that are both effective and practical Singapore adopts a similar approach given the rapid advancement of AI since then. We embarked on a public consultation on AI and IP this year. The Swiss IPI is a collaborator on the patent issues in our consultation. Our public consultation was shaped through co-creation with a broad range of domestic and international stakeholders.
Additionally, we are concurrently working with WIPO on a pilot AI licensing scoping study in Singapore. The study will help Singapore assess the feasibility of emerging licensing opportunities for AI-related users of copyright works. As we continue our work in this area, diverse perspectives will be critical to developing inclusive policy responses and calibrating our IP framework. AI will continue to evolve and transform how we innovate and create. Our IP systems and policies must evolve in tandem to remain relevant, trusted, and responsive.
In charting the way ahead, Singapore reaffirms our commitment to engaging and collaborating internationally, including through the various WIPO Standing Committees. We welcome Member States and organizations to work with us in exchanging insights and developing shared solutions and joint initiatives that respect and respond to different national circumstances and priorities Thank you. Thank you very much.
