# 발표자: CANADA  (floor)
# Day 1 · 2026-11-09 · 예정 17:25-17:55 (Geneva) · 발언 1회 · 4단락 · 444단어 · 구분 방식: official

Thank you, Mr. President. Canada thanks the Secretariat for organizing this first global forum on Intellectual Property and Artificial Intelligence as well as the Distinguished Delegates who are sharing their experience. These exchanges will be valuable for the Canadian Intellectual Property Office as we examine how AI is transforming innovation and the systems of Intellectual Property and how national offices can use these technologies responsibly to improve their services. Earlier this year, Canada launched AI for All. Our national AI strategy is focused on trust, opportunity and sovereignty.
In line with this priority given to sovereignty, the government of Canada has announced on the 29th of October a capital investment of up to $1.6 billion in COHERIN, to help deploy secure and sovereign AI in Canada. Combined with initiatives like the AI Computing Access Fund, this investment demonstrates the importance of Canada places on strengthening its national AI capability, also reducing the risks associated with the concentration of cutting edge AI capabilities in the hands of a small number of companies and countries Canada is at the forefront of AI research. Of a small number of companies and countries, Canada is at the forefront of AI research and now it’s about transforming this strength into adoption, commercialization and economic value. As AI transforms the way innovation happens, we are looking to see if current national frameworks continue to provide appropriate incentives for innovation and dissemination and how the country is interacting with data related issues, competition, security and sovereignty. We have established a CIPO wide emerging technology working group and an AI working group dedicated to our patent examination functions and we are developing an organizational AI strategy covering prioritization, risk assessment, governance and training.
We already have practical experience. CIPO uses AI enabled tools to support industrial design prior art searches and trademark examination which have proven to improve the quality of the work and reduce time spent to accomplish those tasks. At the same time, responsible deployment matters. As we explore further applications including patent classification and prior art searching, we are considering data security, confidentiality, reliability and quality, human oversight and government of Canada requirements for responsible AI usage and automated decision making. Finally, many broader IP questions raised by AI remain unsettled.
We are particularly interested in learning from other offices about AI assisted invention and creation, changing filing behaviors, AI generated prior art, training data, attribution and the protection and commercialization of AI technologies. Canada appreciates WIPO’s role in bringing together governments, IP offices, innovators and other stakeholders to share evidence and practical experience. CIPO looks forward to continuing these exchanges and sharing our experience as our work progresses.
