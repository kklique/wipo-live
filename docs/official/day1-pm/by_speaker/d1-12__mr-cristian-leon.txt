And for recognizing DROVID as a winner of this year’s Global Awards, I would like to start with a question. What if we could have a fire before it even starts? This is not a theoretical question. In February of 2024, a forest fire spread to the houses and 134 people died. I asked myself that question long before that.
In 2014, I hiked through the Torres del Paine in Chilean Patagonia. There were more than 17,000 hectares burned. That fire was caused by negligence. Later during the pandemic, I had the opportunity to investigate and see how to stop a fire before it starts. And I founded DROVID at the end of 2020.
Every forest fire starts with a person. Around the world, about 9 out of 10 do. In Chile, almost all of them. This is a stage that is often not explored enough. However, we operate from a specific perspective in terms of time, and that perspective has not been sufficiently explored.
It is within this window that a large proportion of fires occur. And right now, we are looking for ways to mitigate that risk from that stage. Most wildfires start with a person. However, the current system and the technologies we have built operate from the moment the fire starts, not from this stage. Before the fire starts, there is a person doing something dangerous in a risky location.
And that is the stage we are addressing. Now, a drone with extended autonomy, with specialized optics, they patrol forests day and night. Artificial intelligence is built into the drone, not in the cloud. Each image is analyzed from the sky in real time. The first model we developed detects fire and smoke from the very moment the fire is very small.
A small camp fire, a small fire seen from the air by the onboard artificial intelligence. And at night, a small fire. That’s when it goes unnoticed. The second model we developed was designed to detect people and vehicles in high-risk areas. By design, what we do is detect presence, not identity.
All automatic detection presents a major challenge. False alarms, an alert, an alarm is usually a fire or smoke visible from several kilometers away. In practice, this is something that often when resources are available or those who have them decide to take action, an immediate one, often without fully understanding what is happening, sending aircraft out immediately. Those who don’t have them, those who don’t have the resources wait until they see what happens. Both decisions are reasonable.
However, one is expensive and the other may be too late. The third model we are developing targets human risk. It is designed to prevent it and to give confidence to those who receive the alerts. Before the fire, there is a stage and we go through this stage in a place that is not a laboratory. It is a real forest.
The data we needed to build this did not exist. So we went to the forest to create them with the help of CONAF, the national forestry corporation and the PDI, the investigative police. We had full support to go to the field to gather that data. We conducted multiple tests and we captured the data we needed to train our models. Both of those institutions validated our way of operating and the scope of the detections.
Since the end of 2024, we have been working with forestal Santa Lucia and a Chilean company. In a territory of 2500 hectares on this land, we have carried out multiple tests and in them our model detected autonomously and the alert reached our team’s phones within a few seconds. It wasn’t a real account. It was a test account. It wasn’t an emergency but it worked.
Following that, the company signed a purchase agreement subject to validation and the next stage, that stage has already begun with the support of a grant from the Chilean government. Since we are at the WIPO, let’s talk a bit about Intellectual Property, our initial developments, our technology. A part of it was born in a laboratory through a collaboration with INRA. The French research institute, they transferred 100% of their ownership to Droviet of the rights to what was developed. We protected what we built in different ways.
We used patents, patent applications and currently they are being processed in 42 countries thanks to the PCT system. We use trade secrets for our training data and our know-how along with copyrights and trademarks. For a small company, this is not bureaucracy. It is key to be able to collaborate with large companies and to not lose what has been built and to be able to take a local solution to other countries. The fire that starts with one person is not just in Chile.
It happens throughout the Mediterranean basin. It happens in California, in India, in Brazil. That’s where we are going. Let’s go back to the question from the beginning. What if we could stop a fire before it starts?
We already know that our technology works. The next step is to be able to detect the next stage. We are making progress on that path. For this, we need and we are looking for areas where we can test it, institutions that support it and people who believe in this, that preventing a fire is worth more than reacting to it. That is our invitation.
That is what we are looking for today at Droid. Let’s move on and let’s move forward towards that fire that never existed. Thank you very much.
