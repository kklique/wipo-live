I think that the I want to pick up from where from the issue of collective, collectively created work because I did mention that there’s a lot more collective, collaborative, communal, you know, creative processes that happen across African societies. The thing with collective licensing in that sense is that in and of itself, collective licensing is not a problem. It’s not a risk. The risk lies in the governance of collective management organizations, collective licensing solutions themselves. If you look across the African continent, what you will find is that collective licensing has given us a better solution in terms of people being able to directly reach creators, directly reach inventors, directly reach innovators because now there’s more, there’s higher bargaining power for you to be able to negotiate as a collective to say this is what we want or this is what we don’t want.
Where things have gone wrong is in the governance, in the accountability, in the transparency of those organizations in how they handle revenue or monies being paid to them to now give over to their members. The second thing also is that collective societies have also not given themselves to, there’s need for more infrastructure in terms of resources that enable them to be better at metadata, be better at tagging, be better at managing the vast volume of creative and inventive data.
