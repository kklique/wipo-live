At CIPO, we are also looking at how AI can improve IP administration. We have established a CIPO -wide emerging technology working group and an AI working group that is to our patent examination functions, and we are developing an organizational AI strategy covering prioritization, risk assessment, governance, and training. We already have practical experience. CIPO uses AI -enabled tools to support industrial -designed prior art searches and trademark examination, which I have proven to improve the quality of the work and reduce time spent to accomplish those tasks.
At the same time, responsible deployment matters. As we explore further applications, including patent classification and prior art searching, we are considering data security, confidentiality, reliability, and quality, human oversight and government of Canada requirements for responsible AI usage and automated decision making. Finally, many broader IP questions raised by AI remain unsettled.
We are particularly interested in learning from other offices about AI assisted invention and changing filing behaviors, AI generated prior art, training data, attribution and the protection and commercialization of AI technologies. Canada appreciates WIPO's role in bringing together governments, IP offices, innovators and other stakeholders to share evidence and practical experience.
