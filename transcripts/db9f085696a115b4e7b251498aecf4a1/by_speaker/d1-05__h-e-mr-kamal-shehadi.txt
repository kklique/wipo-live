Thank you very much for your attention. Director General, Daren Tang, Excellencies, Distinguished Delegates, ladies and gentlemen, it is an honor to join you at the first Global Forum on Intellectual Property and Artificial Intelligence.
I am sorry I cannot be with you in Geneva, but I am glad to open this conversation with you from Beirut. I would like to thank WIPO and Director General Tang for creating this space. Artificial intelligence is moving faster than most legal frameworks were built to handle. A forum that brings Member States and stakeholders together regularly and openly is exactly what this moment needs. The questions before you are not small.
They are the questions of the age. Who is the author of the work created with AI?
Who is the discoverer of something an algorithm help discover. What happens to the rights of creators whose work is used to train these systems? Each answer will shape how people create, invest and share knowledge for decades to come. In Lebanon, we live and deal with these questions from a particular vantage point. We are a country with a long tradition of creativity, entrepreneurship and talent.
Our engineers designers, writers, and founders around the world. At the same time, we are building our own digital foundations and developing our approach to AI at home. So we know that Intellectual Property cannot be an afterthought. It is what lets a young innovator in Beirut, Tripoli or Saida, believe that an idea can become a business and that the business can grow without the idea being taken from them.
We also know that no country can answer these questions alone. Large economies and smaller ones, those that build AI and those that mostly use AI. all have a stake in shaping the rules. Smaller countries need a voice in shaping those rules and not only the task of adhering to them. This is why forums like this one matter. I would like to leave you with three hopes for the next two days.
