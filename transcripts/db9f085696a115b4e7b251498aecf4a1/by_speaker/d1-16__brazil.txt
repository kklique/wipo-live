# 발표자: BRAZIL  (floor)
# Day 1 · 2026-11-09 · 예정 17:25-17:55 (Geneva) · 발언 1회 · 6문장 · 397단어 · 구분 방식: marker

Over the past years, artificial intelligence has been reshaping the world with implications for the Intellectual Property system, societies and states. copyright, including against the risks of algorithmic bias and vendor lock-in associated access.
The inclusion of a case study of a Latin American SME in the program today is far from sufficient to address the developed dimension of AI. We must ensure that Developing Countries are able to participate meaningfully in the development of AI technologies rather than remaining mere consumers of solutions developed elsewhere. Equally important is ensuring affordable access to the existing technologies, including by considering the use of flexibilities provided for in the international IP regime.
The 2026 UNCTAD's Trade and Development Report illustrates the magnitude of this goal. Global investment in AI remains heavily concentrated in developed countries, accounting for 70 % of the total. Moreover, the UNCTAD report points to a widening gap between developed and developing countries in new technology manufacturing, both in terms of research and development as well as market capture.
WIPO is a specialized agency of the United Nations and as such has a responsibility to contribute to the implementation of the 2030 Agenda. and its Sustainable Development Goals. Discussions on AI and IP cannot be detached from these broader objectives, nor should they be shaped only by the perspectives of technology providers. This morning debate showed from one side the pervasive impact of AI on rights holders and content creators, their demand for compliance and the overbroad assessment that the IP system is not fit for the challenge.
On the other side, we testify to the lack of goodwill to comply with the obligation imposed by the IP system. the attempt to change rights holder focus from a negative to a positive one.
That is to say from generative AI to commercial platforms and digital distribution services. The morning debate only made the case for Brazil and other Member States on the pressing and urgent need for dealing with the AI issue at the institutional venues, the WIPO committees such as the SCCR and the IGC. The disruptive nature of AI is the strongest call for a faithful assessment where this organization and its Member States stand when it comes to the balance that the IP system needs to strike between exclusive rights and the broader societal benefits of innovation, access to knowledge and technological progress.
