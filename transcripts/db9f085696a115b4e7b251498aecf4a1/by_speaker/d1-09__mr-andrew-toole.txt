If patent and scientific data could have helped us anticipate the AI revolution, what might they be telling us about the next breakthrough technology?
Our next insight, painting by numbers, the power of IP data, explores how IP information can offer not just a record of past innovations, but a window into its future.
To help us connect the dots, please welcome to the stage Mr. Andrew Toole, Associate Professor of Public Policy at the Georgia Institute of Technology. Andrew, the floor is yours. Good afternoon, everyone. and thank you to the organizers for this opportunity. I appreciate it very much. I think all of us here really understand the potential of new technologies to fundamentally change our daily lives, our job opportunities, and even our interpersonal relationships.
So, let's think about, can, could we actually identify new technologies as they emerge? If we can do that, we are going to change our perspective from being retrospective to prospective. That is to say, when we have a prospective... understanding, we have the opportunity to shape the ecosystem to provide the policies, the incentives, and the infrastructure that will define and allow us to harness the benefits of the new technology.
If we are proactive, we are also able to set up governance structures if those are necessary to protect people from the new technology. And, of course, if we are proactive, we can have our IP offices understand the staffing needs that they are going to need so that they can continue to have quality examination and low pendency. So the question is, excuse me, the question is can IP data help us to be proactive by allowing us to identify emerging technologies early?
My answer is yes. So the patent data are indispensable source for identifying emerging technologies. However, they cannot be used in isolation. The key is detecting the language of discovery in science and in industry. that will fuel early and follow on inventions. So to illustrate this point, I would like to go through the case of the emergence of generative AI, and we first we know that it was probably an amazing eye-opening experience in November of 2022 when chat GPT burst onto the scene.
It was. And so the question is, could we have anticipated the that bursting of chat GPT? Well, today we have the, you know, the power of hindsight, the 2020 vision that we can look back in time, we can see what actually happened and we can learn lessons from that information. So here is what the US patent data we are showing. Each bar counts the US patent applications that named generative AI model, things like generative adversarial network, variational autoencoder, or transformer.
So for years the count was near zero. but then in 2017 and 2018, it turns up. 12 applications in 2017. 67 applications in 2018. almost 200 applications in 2019, and nearly 600 by 2022. So by the time chat GPT arrived, these filings had been rising for about five years, a half of a decade. So after chat GPT exploded, onto the scene, there were more than 4,600 applications in 2025 for generative AI.
So the signal was there and it was actually in plain sight. So let me put this on a timeline. The inventions behind the first rise were filed in 2016 and 2017, and most patents have an 18 -month period before they are made public. and so we see the rise publicly in 2018. Now look at what came next. In January 2019, as was just mentioned, WIPO published its technology trends report on artificial intelligence, an excellent report.
The patent coverage of that report extended only through early of 2018. Now WIPO names in that report, deep learning, neural networks, as the fastest growing AI techniques, but there's no mention of generative adversarial networks in that document. The key GAN, that is the short, the key GAN paper was nearly four years old when the data were pulled for that report. Georgetown University followed up quickly on what WIPO had done, an excellent report, in their Center for Security and Emerging Technology with a consulting company, 1790 Analytics.

And just weeks after that, in January of 2023, both the IPC, the International Patent Classification and the CPC classification system added a code for generative AI. and WIPO published this landscape on generative AI in July of 2024.
So the gold band in the graphic here represents nearly five years when the signal was public if we had known what to look for. And so why did not we see it?
