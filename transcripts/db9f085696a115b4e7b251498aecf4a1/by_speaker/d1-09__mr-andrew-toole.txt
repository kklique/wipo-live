Our next insight, painting by numbers, the power of IP data, explores how IP information can offer not just a record of past innovations, but a window into its future.
To help us connect the dots, please welcome to the stage Mr. Andrew Toole, Associate Professor of Public Policy at the Georgia Institute of Technology. Andrew, the floor is yours. Good afternoon, everyone. and thank you to the organizers for this opportunity. I appreciate it very much. I think all of us here really understand the potential of new technologies to fundamentally change our daily lives, our job opportunities, and even our interpersonal relationships.
So, let's think about, can, could we actually identify new technologies as they emerge? If we can do that, we are going to change our perspective from being retrospective to prospective. That is to say, when we have a prospective... understanding, we have the opportunity to shape the ecosystem to provide the policies, the incentives, and the infrastructure that will define and allow us to harness the benefits of the new technology.
If we are proactive, we are also able to set up governance structures if those are necessary to protect people from the new technology. And, of course, if we are proactive, we can have our IP offices understand the staffing needs that they are going to need so that they can continue to have quality examination and low pendency. So the question is, excuse me, the question is can IP data help us to be proactive by allowing us to identify emerging technologies early?
My answer is yes. So the patent data are indispensable source for identifying emerging technologies. However, they cannot be used in isolation. The key is detecting the language of discovery in science and in industry. that will fuel early and follow on inventions. So to illustrate this point, I would like to go through the case of the emergence of generative AI, and we first we know that it was probably an amazing eye-opening experience in November of 2022 when chat GPT burst onto the scene.
