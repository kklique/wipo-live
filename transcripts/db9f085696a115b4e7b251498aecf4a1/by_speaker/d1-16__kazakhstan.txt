So now I give the floor to Ms. Lazard. Utesheva, Deputy Director of CAS Patent, Kazakhstan. She is on, I believe, over Internet. Yes. Thank you. Mr. Chair. Distinguished Okay. Today we heard many times that AI should keep the human at the center. I would like to share a practical example of how we try to do this in Kazakhstan. The the National Intellectual Property Office, we are steadily developing our digital systems.
We have automated many of our services and we already use AI in the examination of trademarks inventions and other IP applications. It helps our experts work faster and more consistently. But AI is also working for us in copyright. Kazakhstan has launched a unified digital platform for copyright. cascopyrightkz one of the places where creators register their works, users obtain licenses and royalties are collected and distributed.
For many years, the main problem in music royalties was simple. Nobody knew exactly what was played. Distribution was based on incomplete reports and estimates. In practice, this means that well-known works were overcounted and local and less famous authors were often not counted at all. AI helps us change this in three ways. First, music recognition. Each recording has a unique digital fingerprint.
This is the system listens to broadcasts, recognizes each track and records what was played, where and when. The second is automated monitoring. Today the platform monitors more than 130 TV and radio channels around the clock. And from the 2027, it will cover more than 1,000 users across Kazakhstan. It comprises what was actually broadcast with what users reported and shows any gaps.
