Although I cannot join you in person, I am pleased to have this opportunity to share Korea's experience and policies on this topic. The first is improving IP administration and services through AI. Our ministry is moving towards AI-based examination systems. using specialized AI models developed in-house. We have already introduced AI into patent such as trademark classification, and design image searches to improve examination quality and will continue to expand its use to other areas.
To make IP information more accessible to the public, we also plan to introduce an AI-powered assistant that explains complex patent documents in plain language. The second is responding to the growing number of AI -assisted IP applications. AI has significantly lowered the barriers to filing IP applications. In the first half of 2026, patent filings by individuals in Korea increased by 130% compared to last year. However, as filing becomes easier, we are also seeing rise in low quality or fraudulent IP applications. involving the misuse of AI, referred to as IP slab. love.
To address this problem, our ministry has introduced an early screening system that detects such applications and sends out alerts. We also plan to review our fee system for individual applicants to discourage excessive filings resulting from low application costs. In addition, we will pursue legal and institutional reforms to address the misuse of AI during the application process. such as the manipulation of experimental data.
These measures will include invalidating patterns and imposing financial penalties for such misconduct. And lastly we are preparing our IP system for the future of AI. Our ministry will look ahead to the changes AI will bring to the way we work. assess how it will affect our organizational structure and administrative operations. and develop strategies accordingly. We also intend to establish the basic principles for AI in intellectual property.
