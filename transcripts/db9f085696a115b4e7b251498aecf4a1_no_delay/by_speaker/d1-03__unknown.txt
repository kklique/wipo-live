Who is an inventor when AI makes a material contribution to an invention? Who is the author when human creativity and machine generation become increasingly intertwined? On what basis and under what conditions should corporate protected material be used for AI training? What are the remuneration and compensation models?
How can we say we respect copyright and yet pay nothing for it? How should we think about software, algorithms and data as sources of economic value? And we are now confronting something even more fundamental. What are the attributes of human identity itself? Our voices, our images, our likenesses, our biometric characteristics, even our thoughts. AI can replicate these in ways that earlier generations could barely have imagined.
As Isaac Asimov said, or was it Arthur C. Clark.
When technology is sufficiently advanced, it is indistinguishable from magic.
Yet, the legal protections surrounding them remain fragmented across IP, privacy, personality rights, unfair competition and other bodies of law.
Indeed, this afternoon the Forum will ask directly whether the IP system has a role in protecting not only what humans make but what makes us human. I do not pretend that any of these questions has an easy answer. but not here just to tackle easy questions. Second, how should the IP system itself change? even if we conclude that the basic architecture of IP remains sound, there is another challenge.
The system of IP registration and protection which we must remember is a starting point for IP commercialization. has to operate at the speed of the innovators and creators and the economies and societies that it serves. Much of today's IP regime and infrastructure was built around industrial innovation cycles measured in the lifespan of years. But digital innovation cycles operates much faster, and AI will accelerate the these cycles even further.
Imagine an entrepreneur building a company whose AI -enabled technology fundamentally changes several times during the period in which an IP right is being examined. Imagine IP offices confronted with vastly increased volumes of AI -assisted applications, by the way, it is already reality, or bodies of machine-generated prior art. Imagine creators trying to license millions of machine-mediated users through transaction models designed for another era.
The question is therefore not only whether we are able to protect what is valuable in the age of AI, but also whether the machinery of IP protection is fast enough, accessible enough and adaptable enough for the AI -enabled innovators and creators it now has to serve. If we do not get this right, we will find ourselves in a situation where innovators and creators will find that the IP system simply does not serve them .
to have a frank conversation about what new processes, timelines and procedures need to be put in place for the IP system to remain relevant in a world where innovation and business cycles move not just in years but increasingly in months and weeks. Third, how do we make the economics work? Perhaps this is the most practical question of all. AI depends on enormous quantities of knowledge, culture, information and data.
That value has been created by human beings and creators rightly want recognition sustainable livelihoods. At the same time, innovators and AI companies need the ability to develop and train powerful models. Researchers need room to experiment. Businesses need predictable and workable rules. Consumers want access to better and more affordable products and services. And society needs both continued human creativity and continue technological innovation.
These interests will sometimes conflict as they while enabling the future generation of creators to emerge. So the question cannot simply be who has the legal power to say no. We must also ask how can we create workable ways for people to say yes. That means exploring new licensing practices, new remuneration mechanisms, better metadata and rights information, technical solutions for attribution and provenance, and new business relationships between creators, rights holders and AI developers.
That is one reason why WIPO created the AI infrastructure interchange in March this year, providing a neutral space where creators, rights holders, technology developers and technical experts can work on practical questions including metadata, watermarking, identifiers and rights management infrastructure.
