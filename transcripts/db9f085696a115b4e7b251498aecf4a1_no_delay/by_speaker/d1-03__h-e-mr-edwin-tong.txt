# [d1-03] Ministerial-level Remarks
# 발표자: H.E. Mr. Edwin Tong  (floor)
# Day 1 · 2026-11-09 · 예정 10:15-10:45 (Geneva) · 발언 1회 · 11문장 · 623단어 · 구분 방식: marker

Thank you very much. Director General Daren Tang, excellencies, ladies and gentlemen. I am sorry I can not join you in person. Unfortunately, for all its advances, AI still cannot put me in two different places. at the same time. But thankfully, technology allows me to deliver this pre-recorded speech, and I am delighted to share some thoughts with you. First, I thank WIPO for convening this important inaugural global forum.
AI is fundamentally transforming how we create and innovate. challenging our very basic assumptions underlying our IP systems. I think we cannot nor should we stand in the way of progress.
As policymakers, our task is to ensure that AI and IP reinforce rather than undermine each other so that innovation and human creativity can thrive together. I would like to suggest three areas where we can work together. First, sustaining human creativity alongside AI innovation. AI models learn from vast quantities of human -created content, whilst increasingly generating content themselves. How do we encourage AI innovation?
without weakening incentives for human creativity? Even before generative AI became mainstream, our copyright Act in Singapore already permitted computational data analysis, including AI training, using lawfully accessed works, subject to safeguards. Our approach is to regulate with a light touch. particularly when technology is evolving so rapidly. This, we believe, encourages innovation.
We also believe in giving creators and innovators the space to experiment, develop new ideas, and bring them to market without being constrained by overly prescriptive rules. At the same time, they must have the confidence that their works are protected. That is the environment we are seeking to provide in Singapore, one that encourages innovation underpinned by a strong, predictable IP framework. But getting this balance right is an ongoing process.
That is why we recently launched a public consultation on AI and IP. We are seeking views on strengthening our legal framework. including how copyright should apply to AI training and outputs. how best to recognize human creativity in AI assisted works, and how our patent framework should respond to AI assisted inventions. I believe these are challenges all of us face.
and also all of us can tackle them together. For example, we can explore practical approaches to licensing and rights management that facilitate lawful AI development. protect creators ' rights, and value human creativity. Second, making different national approaches work together. Countries will understandably adopt different approaches. but fragmented rules could create legal uncertainty and opportunities for regulatory arbitrage.
As a small open economy, Singapore places particular importance on a predictable interoperable, international IP system. We need not agree on everything, but we can find common ground and make our different approaches work better together. Singapore is keen to contribute our experience. Through WIPO, we can explore common principles, develop guidance on different national approaches, and find practical solutions.
to cross-border challenges. Third, ensuring AI expands opportunities for everyone. AI could give individuals and small businesses capabilities once reserved for larger organizations. Yet, concentrated control over data, computing power, and IP could limit these opportunities. We must therefore guard against anti-competitive practices that exclude smaller players whilst helping them acquire the knowledge and expertise to harness AI and manage their IP effectively.
Singapore has long supported international capacity building in IP.
This April, for example, we partnered with PO to organize an executive program on emerging AI and IP challenges, bringing together professionals from nine countries. As Singapore assumes the ASEAN Championship next year, we look forward to building on such initiatives. to help more businesses and creators harness AI. Our long-standing partnerships with WIPO and IP offices around the world. reflect our belief that we achieve more by learning from one another and then combining our strengths.
