Artificial intelligence is moving faster than most legal frameworks were built to handle. WIPO is a forum that brings Member States and stakeholders together regularly and openly, is exactly what this moment needs.
The questions before you are not small. They are the questions of the who is the author of a work created with AI. Who is the discoverer of something an algorithm helped discover? What happens to the rights of creators whose work is used to train these systems? Each answer will shape how people create, invest, and share knowledge for decades to come. In Lebanon, we live and deal with these questions from a particular vantage point.
We are a country with a long tradition of creativity. entrepreneurship and talent. Our engineers, designers, writers and founders work around the world.
At the same time, we are building our own digital foundations and developing our approach to AI at home. So we know that Intellectual Property cannot be an afterthought. It is what lets a young innovator in Beirut, Tripoli or Saida believe that an idea can become a business and that the business can grow without the idea being taken. from them. We also know that no country can answer these questions alone.
Large economies and smaller ones, those that build AI and those that mostly use AI, all have a stake in shaping the rules.
Smaller countries need a voice in shaping those rules and not only the task of adhering to them. This is why forums like this one matter.
